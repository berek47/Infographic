Leakage represents cash held outside the banking system or reserves not re-lent. It shrinks the real-world multiplier.

The takeaway

A $1,000 deposit can support $10,000 of money supply at a 10% reserve ratio. The reserve ratio is the dial.

The dial

A lower reserve ratio means more of each deposit is lent forward.

The formula

The maximum money supply equals the original deposit divided by the reserve ratio.

The limits

Cash leakage and unlent reserves make the practical multiplier smaller.

§1The multiplier

In the clean textbook case, every loan is re-deposited and each bank lends everything not held as reserve.

Maximum money supply = Deposit ÷ Reserve ratio (1)

The bars shrink because every new round starts from the previous loan, not from the original deposit.

§2Why the real world is smaller

The live leakage slider adds a practical drag. If households hold cash or banks choose not to lend, the cascade stops sooner.
